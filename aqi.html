The Air Quality Index (AQI) assigns a score to different areas indicating the cleanliness of the air.Thus, A lower score reflects superior air quality, which often correlates with increased utilization of clean energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power. These energy forms release minimal to no pollutants into the atmosphere, contributing significantly to cleaner air. This shift not only enhances environmental health but also public health, reducing diseases associated with air pollution.

AQI scores range from 0 to 500. The higher the AQI value, the greater the level of air pollution and the greater the health concern. For example, an AQI value of 50 represents good air quality with little potential to affect public health, while an AQI value over 300 represents hazardous air quality.The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a crucial public health tool used to measure and report daily air quality. It informs the public about how clean or polluted the air is, and what associated health effects might be a concern. The index scales from 0 to 500, where higher values mean greater air pollution levels and higher health risks. It categorizes air quality into six levels, from 'Good' to 'Hazardous,' each providing guidance on the potential impact on people's health. Understanding and utilizing the AQI helps individuals make informed decisions about their outdoor activities, especially in areas prone to pollution.
